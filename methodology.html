How we calculate your footprint

Every number in the app comes from a published dataset. Here's which ones, how they're combined, and where the estimates are weakest.

The basic sum

kg CO₂e = what you did × conversion to a standard unit × emission factor

For example, 30 minutes in a medium petrol car is about 20 km (at an assumed 40 km/h), and 20 km × 0.174 kg per km gives 3.5 kg CO₂e. “CO₂e” (carbon dioxide equivalent) folds methane and nitrous oxide into one figure, weighted by how strongly each warms the planet over 100 years (IPCC AR5 values, as used by the EPA and UK sources).

When you log something, its kg is calculated on the server and fixed at that moment, so later changes to your settings or to the data don't rewrite your history.

Comparing you with companies

The home page sets your last 30 days against what large companies emit in an average month (their latest yearly figure ÷ 12). Your month is the unit: one cube, the size of that CO₂ as a gas at 15 °C and sea-level pressure (1.87 kg per cubic metre). A block is a million of your cubes (100 × 100 × 100), and each company is drawn as a pile of blocks: its monthly emissions ÷ your month ÷ 1,000,000. Every pile uses the same block size, and a part-block is drawn smaller by volume. Because the unit is your own month, a lower footprint makes every company's pile bigger. The companies count different things, so read it as a sense of scale, not a league table: the oil producers include the emissions of everyone who burns the fuel they sell, while the others report their own operations, the electricity they buy and their supply chains (“scopes 1, 2 and 3”).

Automatic tracking

  • Green Button files (from your utility) are added up per day in your local time zone, using the time zone in the file. Daylight saving time isn't applied, so an hour of usage near midnight can land on the neighbouring day. Electricity uses your grid; gas uses the UK natural gas factor (1 therm = 29.3 kWh). Electricity you send back to the grid (for example from solar panels) isn't subtracted.
  • Odometer readings log the distance since your previous reading on the day of the new reading.
  • Trip logs are added up per day. Importing a file again replaces those days' imported totals.

Limits worth knowing

  • These are averages. Your actual car, home, diet and grid can be well above or below them.
  • The transport figures (other than your own car) come from the UK, whose cars, buses and trains differ somewhat from US ones.
  • Sources draw their boundaries differently (exhaust only, farm to retail, farm to plate), so compare totals between categories loosely.
  • AI summaries only word suggestions; the totals and every saving they quote are calculated by the app.
  • When AI turns a description of your day into entries, it can only pick activities and foods from this database, the app calculates every kg, and nothing is saved until you check the list and add it. Its assumptions (like a 5-minute trip meaning 10 minutes of driving there and back) are shown on each item.
